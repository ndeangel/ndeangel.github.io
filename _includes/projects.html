Research Projects

CUSP

The Cubesat Solar Polarimeter (CUSP) is a 6U-XL CubeSat mission aiming to measure the polarization of hard X-rays (25-100 keV) emitted by solar flares during which a large amount of energy is released from the Sun in the form of electromagnetic radiation and charged particles. Charged particles are accelerated toward Earth by solar flares and the often associated Coronal Mass Ejections, causing damage to electronic devices in orbit (satellites) and on Earth. CUSP will contribute to the study of the physical processes at play at the flare production sites, but also of the emission and acceleration of energetic charged particles towards our planet, making it a formidable forecasting tool for space weather.

POLAR-2

POLAR-2 is a large-scale gamma-ray polarimeter based on the legacy of the POLAR experiment. It is currently being developed for a targeted launch early 2025 to the China Space Station (CSS) for a nominal operation time of 2 years. With a 4 times bigger instrument than its predecessor, and technological upgrades improving the effective area at low energies, POLAR-2 aims to provide high quality joint polarization-spectral measurements of about 50 GRBs every year. The higher statistics will allow detailed time and energy dependent polarization analyses of these GRBs' prompt emission.

SpaceItUp!

Space It Up! is a national research initiative funded by the Italian Space Agency (ASI) and the Ministry of University and Research (MUR). It brings together leading Italian universities, research centres and companies within a coordinated consortium that aims to strengthen Italy’s scientific and technological capabilities in the rapidly evolving space sector.

Past Projects

PHEMTO

The Polarimetric High Energy Modular Telescope Observatory (PHEMTO) was a medium-class mission proposed for the ESA M8 call, designed to perform imaging, spectroscopy, timing, and polarimetry of high energy sources with unprecedented capabilities in the 1-400 keV band. PHEMTO aimed to answer the following scientific questions: What is the role of magnetic field in our galaxy? How accretion links to ejection in accreting black hole and neutron stars systems? How to constrain explosion physics in supernovae and their remnants? How is the inner region of obscured AGN structured and what is their demography? What is the non-thermal X-ray energy content of clusters of galaxies?

EXPO

The Enhanced X-ray Polarimetry Observatory (EXPO) was a mission proposed to the ESA M8 call, aiming to drastically improve the sensitivity of current X-ray polarimeters while extending the energy band probed to fully explore the astrophysical potential of hard X-ray polarimetry.

POLAR

POLAR was a compact Compton polarimeter located on the Tiangong-2 Chinese space lab dedicated to the measurement of Gamma-Ray Burst (GRB) prompt emission polarization. It took scientific data for about 6 months starting September 2016 and detected 55 GRBs as well as several pulsars and solar flares. Joint polarization and spectral analysis lead to the conclusion that most of the detected GRBs were consistent with unpolarized or lowly polarized sources, excluding synchrotron emission models with ordered magnetic fields. Time resolved analysis showed a hint for a time evolving polarization angle within a GRB single pulse, washing out the polarization fraction on time integrated studies.

eXTP

The enhanced X-ray Timing and Polarimetry mission (eXTP) is a science mission designed to study the state of matter under extreme conditions of density, gravity and magnetism. Primary goals are the determination of the equation of state of matter at supra-nuclear density, the measurement of QED effects in highly magnetized star, and the study of accretion in the strong-field regime of gravity. Primary targets include isolated and binary neutron stars, strong magnetic field systems like magnetars, and stellar-mass and supermassive black holes.

SMILE

SMILE (Student Moon Initiative for Lunar Exploration) is an international graduate-student-led space mission with a team of mostly doctoral students. The goal of our mission is to land a small (CubeSat-sized) spacecraft that conducts meaningful science on the surface of the moon by 2024. The objective of the primary payloads is to perform in-situ measurements of the radiation environment on the lunar surface and to characterize the behavior of radiation resistant biological organisms for future manned lunar exploration.

LEAP

The LargE Area Burst Polarimeter (LEAP) will radically improve our understanding of some of the most energetic phenomena in our Universe by exposing the underlying physics that governs astrophysical jets and the extreme environment surrounding newborn compact objects. It will consist of a wide FOV Compton polarimeter able to measure GRB polarization over the energy range 50–500 keV and GRB spectrum from 20 keV to 5 MeV. It will be deployed as an external payload on the International Space Station (ISS).

CTA

The Cherenkov Telescope Array (CTA) is the next generation ground-based observatory for gamma-ray astronomy at very-high energies. With more than 100 telescopes located in the northern and southern hemispheres, CTA will be the world’s largest and most sensitive high-energy gamma-ray observatory.
